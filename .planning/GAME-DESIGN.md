# Game Design — The Forgotten Hour

**Game:** The Forgotten Hour  
**Adventure One:** The Salt Archive  
**Genre:** Narrative mystery puzzle adventure  
**Platform:** iPhone and iPad, native SwiftUI  
**Audience:** Players who enjoy atmospheric deduction, environmental storytelling, and fair, hand-authored puzzles  
**Playtime:** 8–10 hours main path; 12–14 hours with optional content  
**Rating target:** 12+ for thematic elements, peril, and depictions of grief; no graphic violence

---

## 1. High Concept

A paper conservator returns to a tidal island town to settle her grandmother's estate and discovers that the entire town agreed to forget one week of its history. By restoring damaged documents, decoding weather records, and reconstructing the night of the Ninth Tide, she uncovers a cover-up that includes her own family — and must decide whether the town is better served by memory or mercy.

The player's fantasy is **the archival detective**: someone who reads the world as a document, notices what has been scratched out, and makes the past speak.

## 2. Design Pillars

1. **Every puzzle is evidence.** A solved puzzle never exists only to gate progress; it changes the player's understanding of a person, place, or event.
2. **The town is the antagonist and the victim.** Morrow's Reach is not haunted by a monster. Its danger is collective silence, politeness, and the comfort of a shared story.
3. **Fair difficulty.** The player should always be able to name the rule they are missing. Hints escalate from a nudge to a method to a full solution, and the story never hard-blocks.
4. **Tactile investigation.** Papers, photographs, tide charts, brass mechanisms, and audio recordings are handled as objects, not menu entries.
5. **Remembering is a choice.** The ending is not a test of skill alone; it is a moral decision earned through investigation.
6. **Designed twice, in two languages.** English and Italian are authored together, with the same clues, tone, and puzzle logic intact.

## 3. Setting and Tone

**Morrow's Reach** is a small tidal island in the North Atlantic, connected to the mainland by a causeway that floods twice a day. In the present day of the story (1997), the town has a shrinking fishing fleet, a shuttered cinema, a clinic that never quite closed, and a lighthouse automation project that nobody wants to finish.

The visual and emotional register is **North Atlantic Gothic**: salt-bleached wood, rust, oilcloth, handwritten ledgers, fog, and rooms that are tidy because someone has been keeping them that way for a long time. There is no supernatural certainty. Strange events have material explanations, but the town's rituals around them are sincere.

The tone is intimate and unsettling rather than gory. The central horror is not what happened; it is how ordinary people maintain a lie that everyone can feel but no one will name.

## 4. Premise

Seventeen years ago, during the town's centennial, a storm surge trapped fourteen children in the undercroft beneath the Tidehouse. The sea gate could have been opened to drain the lower town, but doing so would have destroyed the harbor's fishing fleet and the mayor's redevelopment plan. The council delayed. Seven children drowned.

In the aftermath, Dr. Selma Orr proposed an experimental "quiet treatment": a sedative combined with infrasound from the Tidehouse fog bell, intended to blunt the trauma of survivors and first responders. The treatment cascaded through the town's shared water supply and the bell's resonance. It erased the week from nearly every resident's memory. The official story became a gas leak and a freak storm.

Edda Vale, the town archivist, helped design the cover-up. She believed forgetting was the only mercy available. She also hid the evidence, including the names of the dead, in the archive she spent the rest of her life pretending not to guard.

Iris Vale was one of the children in the undercroft. Her mother, Mira, refused the treatment and fled the island with her before the second session. Iris's memories were never fully erased, only fractured. She has spent her adult life believing the gaps were childhood illness. Edda's death brings her back.

The fog bell still rings at low tide. Cormac Rusk, the lighthouse keeper, has been ringing it by hand.

## 6. Narrative Structure

The game is divided into six chapters, each named for a tide state. Each chapter contains four main puzzles, one optional puzzle, one major location, and at least one conversation that recontextualizes an earlier scene.

### Chapter 1 — Low Water

**Surface goal:** Inventory Edda's house and settle the estate.  
**Investigation:** A locked writing desk, a tide chart with a missing week, a photograph with a child's face scratched out.  
**Puzzles:** observation and object interaction; a simple substitution cipher in Edda's shorthand; aligning a torn photograph; reading a tide table for an impossible date.  
**Revelation:** Iris's own name appears in a 1980 ledger of "persons present" at the Tidehouse.  
**Emotional beat:** The town is kind, but no one will answer a direct question.

### Chapter 2 — The Weir

**Surface goal:** Find the missing archive key and learn why the Tidehouse was closed.  
**Investigation:** Nell's cinema, the harbor office, Tomas's boat, the flooded causeway.  
**Puzzles:** projector film splice; harbor map routing under tide constraints; a bell-pattern listening puzzle; a logic grid over the town's storm records.  
**Revelation:** The official storm record is forged. The lower town was deliberately not drained.  
**Emotional beat:** Tomas remembers a game he should not remember, and pretends he doesn't.

### Chapter 3 — Dead Reckoning

**Surface goal:** Access the clinic archive and identify the "quiet treatment."  
**Investigation:** Dr. Orr's office, the recovery ward, a locked dispensary, the chapel's sealed letters.  
**Puzzles:** a patient ledger logic grid; a dosage cryptarithm; a timeline reconstruction; a visual puzzle using optical lenses and old X-rays.  
**Revelation:** The town was not exposed to gas. It was sedated and exposed to a resonant frequency. The memory loss was engineered.  
**Emotional beat:** Orr is not a monster; she is a person who made a monstrous decision and has been living inside its consequences.

### Chapter 4 — The Ninth Tide

**Surface goal:** Reach the undercroft and reconstruct the night of the flood.  
**Investigation:** the Tidehouse machinery, the sea gate control room, the children's shelter, the mayor's sealed minutes.  
**Puzzles:** a tide-gate rotation mechanism; a constrained scheduling puzzle over the rescue timeline; an acoustic resonance puzzle using the fog bell; a spatial reconstruction of the undercroft.  
**Revelation:** Seven children died. Iris was there. The gate was sabotaged, then the rescue was delayed to hide the sabotage and the harbor deal.  
**Emotional beat:** The town's silence is not a mystery anymore. It is a crime with a guest list.

### Chapter 5 — Salt Lines

**Surface goal:** Find Mira and confront Cormac before the town's Founders' Assembly.  
**Investigation:** the lighthouse, Mira's rented cottage, the salt-line caves, the archive's sealed room.  
**Puzzles:** lighthouse lens optics; a cross-chapter "salt line" map; a false-clue discrimination puzzle; a confession cipher that requires choosing which witness is lying.  
**Revelation:** Cormac sabotaged the gate; Edda helped conceal the deaths; Mira left to protect Iris from the second treatment.  
**Emotional beat:** Every person Iris has trusted is complicit in a different way, and each had a reason that is not enough.

### Chapter 6 — Still Water

**Surface goal:** Decide what the town will remember before the Founders' Assembly.  
**Investigation:** the assembly hall, the archive, the bell tower, the causeway at low tide.  
**Puzzles:** the final "Ledger" puzzle, which requires the player to reconcile conflicting records using every mechanic learned so far; an optional "Keeper's Question" meta-puzzle for the secret ending.  
**Revelation:** The truth can be proven, but it cannot be made painless. The town must choose between a clean reckoning, a merciful silence, or a shared, partial memory.  
**Emotional beat:** Iris stops asking what happened and starts deciding what she owes the dead and the living.

## 7. Truth Layer and Revelation Discipline

This section is spoiler material for the design team. The player should be able to reconstruct every revelation from evidence, but not before the chapter that earns it.

| Question | Answer | First planted | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why did the town lose a week? | A sedative and infrasound treatment cascaded through the water supply and the fog bell | Ch. 2 bell pattern | Ch. 3 clinic archive |
| Was there a gas leak? | No; the gas story was invented to explain the sedation | Ch. 1 newspaper | Ch. 3 medical ledger |
| Why was the lower town not drained? | The mayor delayed opening the sea gate to protect a harbor development deal | Ch. 2 forged storm record | Ch. 4 council minutes |
| Who sabotaged the gate? | Cormac Rusk, trying to stop the marsh development, unaware of the children | Ch. 2 harbor maps | Ch. 5 lighthouse confession |
| Who helped cover it up? | Edda Vale, Dr. Orr, Mayor Venn, and Father Pike (by silence) | Ch. 1 marginalia | Ch. 5 sealed letters |
| Why did Mira leave? | She refused the second treatment and took Iris before it could erase her completely | Ch. 3 photograph | Ch. 5 Mira's account |
| Was Iris one of the children? | Yes; she survived, but her memories were fractured rather than erased | Ch. 1 ledger | Ch. 4 undercroft |
| What happened to the seven? | They drowned in the undercroft when the gate stayed closed | Ch. 2 names on cinema seats | Ch. 4 memorial wall |

**Revelation rules:**

- No revelation may depend on a single missable hotspot. The journal records the supporting evidence even if the player solves a puzzle with a hint.
- A false clue is allowed only if the game later gives the player a fair way to discriminate it from the truth. False clues must be marked in the design bible with their refutation.
- The player should be able to state the central truth in one sentence before the final choice: *the town engineered its own forgetting to hide a preventable drowning.*
- The final choice is moral, not factual. By Chapter 6, the facts are settled; what remains is what to do with them.

## 8. Puzzle Framework

### 8.1 Puzzle contract

Every puzzle must declare:

- A stable ID, chapter, location, difficulty tier, and estimated solve time.
- The evidence it reveals and the journal entry it unlocks.
- The rule the player must discover, stated in one sentence for the hint system.
- A deterministic validator with explicit success and failure states.
- Three hints: **Point**, **Principle**, and **Reveal**.
- An accessibility alternative that preserves the deduction, not just the answer.
- A replay state that allows re-solving without duplicating rewards.

### 8.2 Puzzle families

| Family | Player action | Example | Accessibility alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observation | Inspect, compare, mark differences | Find the altered date in a ledger | List view with described differences; keyboard focus |
| Cipher | Substitute, reorder, decode | Edda's shorthand in a margin note | Text entry with character picker; no timing |
| Logic grid | Cross-reference constraints | Match patients to treatment dates | Stepper-based grid with spoken row/column summaries |
| Spatial | Rotate, align, route | Rebuild the sea-gate mechanism | Rotation buttons and coordinate list; no free drag required |
| Mathematical | Deduce quantities or schedules | Reconstruct the rescue timeline | Numeric keypad and equation builder |
| Audio | Identify patterns or sequences | The fog bell's resonant interval | Visual waveform, captioned tones, and pattern grid |
| Linguistic | Interpret ambiguity or translation | A bilingual note that changes meaning | Full text alternatives and glossary; no audio-only clue |
| Deduction | Choose between contradictory accounts | Decide which witness is lying | Evidence board with explicit premise toggles |
| Meta | Combine rules from multiple chapters | The final Ledger | Structured worksheet with saved intermediate conclusions |

### 8.3 Difficulty ladder

Difficulty is defined by **how many independent insights are required**, not by how obscure the answer is.

| Tier | Chapters | Cognitive load | New mechanic | Fairness target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Beginner | 1 | One obvious rule, one step | Inspect and compare | 90% of playtesters solve without a hint |
| 2. Intermediate | 1–2 | Two rules, one combination | Cipher and logic grid | 70% solve with at most one hint |
| 3. Advanced | 3 | Multi-step deduction with a red herring | Timeline and dosage reasoning | 50% solve with up to two hints |
| 4. Expert | 4 | Interlocking constraints and spatial reasoning | Mechanism and resonance | 35% solve with hints; frustration must stay low |
| 5. Master | 5 | Cross-chapter synthesis and false-clue discrimination | Meta-evidence board | 20% solve unaided; hints make it completable |
| 6. Nearly Impossible | 6 | One fair but non-obvious synthesis of every prior rule | Final Ledger | 10% solve unaided; the solution feels inevitable in retrospect |

**Progression rule:** A new mechanic is introduced in isolation, practiced once with a low-stakes puzzle, then combined with an earlier mechanic. No puzzle may require a mechanic that has not been taught.

### 8.4 Session shape

Each chapter is designed for 60–100 minutes of main-path play, divisible into 10–20 minute sessions:

1. Arrival and environmental storytelling.
2. One conversation that opens a question.
3. Two puzzles that produce evidence.
4. A quiet beat that lets the player form a theory.
5. One puzzle that challenges the theory.
6. A chapter-ending revelation and a save point.

The player can leave at any moment; autosave records the exact scene, puzzle state, inventory, journal, and hints used.

## 9. Hint System

The hint system is a teaching tool, not a monetization mechanic. All hints are available offline and cost nothing.

| Tier | Name | What it gives | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Point | Narrows attention without stating the rule | "The date is not the only thing that changed in this record." |
| 2 | Principle | States the rule or relationship the player needs | "The tide table is consistent only if the missing week is read backward." |
| 3 | Reveal | Shows the next concrete step, then offers to complete the puzzle | Highlights the correct row, then offers "Solve with assistance" |

**Rules:**

- Hints unlock sequentially; a player can request Tier 2 only after Tier 1, and Tier 3 only after Tier 2.
- The journal records that assistance was used, but no ending, achievement, or content is locked behind unaided play.
- After Tier 3, the player may mark the puzzle solved. The evidence and story continue.
- Hint text is localized and written as carefully as puzzle text; it must never insult the player.
- A "Hint history" screen lets the player review what they have already been told.

## 10. Investigation Journal and Case Board

The journal is the player's second brain and the game's memory. It is available from every scene and is fully readable with VoiceOver.

**Tabs:**

- **Case:** current questions, open threads, and chapter progress.
- **People:** portraits, known facts, contradictions, and last-seen location.
- **Places:** map of Morrow's Reach, unlocked locations, tide state, and points of interest.
- **Evidence:** documents, photographs, objects, and audio transcripts.
- **Fragments:** Iris's recovered memories, presented as short illustrated passages.
- **Inventory:** usable objects; combine and inspect actions.

**Case board:** a pin-and-string deduction surface where the player can link two pieces of evidence and label the relationship. The board is optional for progression but required for two optional puzzles and the secret ending. Links are validated against a curated set of meaningful relationships; the game never punishes a speculative link.

**Journal behavior:**

- New entries are marked, never auto-opened over the current scene.
- Every main puzzle adds a plain-language summary of what was learned.
- Contradictions are recorded as questions, not answers, until the player resolves them.
- The journal can generate a spoiler-safe recap when resuming after a long absence.

## 11. Endings

### Ending A — The Clean Tide

Iris delivers the archive to the town assembly. The names of the seven are read aloud. Cormac confesses; Orr refuses to apologize; the harbor deal collapses. The town is fractured, but the record is public. Iris stays to rebuild the archive as a public trust.

**Tone:** costly justice. Not triumphant.

### Ending B — The Mercy Tide

Iris seals the archive and leaves the town its chosen silence. She keeps one copy of the names, not to publish but to remember privately. The town survives, but Iris's relationship with Mira remains unresolved. The final image is the fog bell ringing with no keeper.

**Tone:** ambiguous mercy. Not a punishment for the player.

### Ending C — The Shared Tide (secret)

Available only after all 8 optional puzzles and the Keeper's Question meta-puzzle. Iris convinces the town to adopt a partial, voluntary record: each resident chooses what to remember, and the archive holds the rest in trust. Cormac testifies; Orr is stripped of her license but not exiled; Mira and Iris begin, awkwardly, to talk. The dead are named, but the town is not destroyed.

**Tone:** hard-won reconciliation. The "best" ending still costs something.

**Ending rules:**

- The final choice is explicit, reversible only by replaying Chapter 6.
- No ending is framed as the "wrong" choice; the epilogue reflects the player's reasoning, not a hidden morality score.
- The secret ending requires investigation, not perfection. Hints may be used.

## 12. Art Direction

**Visual identity:** hand-illustrated 2D scenes in ink, gouache, and digital paper grain. The palette is salt white, oxidized teal, rust red, amber lamplight, and deep ink. Color is used for emotional temperature, never as the only carrier of puzzle information.

**Scene construction:**

- Each location is a layered illustration: background sky/sea, midground architecture, foreground props, and a light/fog layer.
- Parallax is subtle and optional. It must be disabled by Reduce Motion.
- Interactive hotspots are drawn into the scene but also exposed as an accessible list.
- Documents and photographs are rendered as readable artifacts, not texture-only props. Text that matters is selectable and screen-reader accessible.
- Transitions evoke physical handling: paper turning, lamplight shifting, tidewater crossing a threshold. No fast flashes or disorienting zooms.

**Character art:** illustrated portraits with a consistent line language, shown during conversations. No photoreal likenesses. Expressions change at narrative beats, not continuously animated.

**UI:** a restrained archival aesthetic: letterpress labels, index tabs, brass rules, and sea-glass accents. Buttons remain obviously tappable and meet contrast requirements.

## 13. Audio Direction

**Music:** an original score built around cello, glass harmonica, prepared piano, and low brass. The score is adaptive: a sparse ambient bed while exploring, a rhythmic pulse during deductions, and a restrained choral texture at revelations. No licensed music.

**Sound design:** fog bell, tide, gulls, wood strain, paper, brass mechanisms, distant radio. Sounds are diegetic where possible and always captioned when they carry information.

**Voice:** no full voice acting at launch. Short non-verbal vocal textures may be used in the score. All dialogue is text-first, with optional auto-advance and adjustable text speed.

**Audio accessibility:**

- Captions for all meaningful sounds, including direction and intensity.
- A visual alternative for every audio puzzle.
- Separate sliders for music, ambience, and effects.
- Audio pauses when the app is backgrounded and resumes without a burst of overlapping sound.
- The game respects the silent switch for sound effects but allows music to continue only if the player explicitly enables it.

## 14. Accessibility and Comfort

Accessibility is a design constraint, not a post-launch patch.

- **VoiceOver:** every scene, hotspot, document, inventory action, puzzle control, and hint is labeled. Puzzles expose their state as structured text.
- **Dynamic Type:** all narrative and UI text scales; documents support a dedicated reading view with adjustable line length.
- **Reduce Motion:** disables parallax, paper-turn animation, fog drift, and non-essential transitions.
- **Color independence:** every color-coded clue also uses shape, pattern, label, or position.
- **Motor access:** no required free-drag, pinch, or precise timing. Rotation, connection, and routing puzzles have button/keyboard alternatives.
- **Audio access:** captions, visual waveforms, and transcripts. No puzzle requires hearing a tone to solve it.
- **Cognitive access:** puzzle rules are restated on demand; hints are always available; the journal summarizes evidence in plain language.
- **Session comfort:** pause anywhere; no fail timers; no penalty for leaving; no flashing imagery.
- **Localization:** layouts are tested with Italian string expansion; no text is baked into images.

## 16. Originality and IP Guardrails

- All names, places, characters, plot events, puzzle texts, and visual designs are original to this project.
- The game may share genre conventions with classic mystery puzzle adventures, but it must not reproduce any existing character, location, puzzle sequence, clue, logo, or distinctive asset.
- Art references are mood and technique only. No asset may be generated "in the style of" a named living artist or an existing franchise.
- Music must be original or licensed with written permission; no temp tracks ship.
- Italian and English scripts are written as parallel originals, not machine-translated at the end.
- Before App Store submission, run a name and trademark search for the final title and subtitle.

## 17. Open Design Questions for Phase 1

These are deliberately unresolved until the vertical slice is played:

1. Does the case board add real deduction or only busywork? Prototype both a required and optional version.
2. Is the final Ledger puzzle satisfying at the "nearly impossible" tier, or does it become a test of patience? Tune with playtest data.
3. Should the secret ending require all optional puzzles, or a smaller set of narrative-critical ones? Avoid making the best ending a completionist grind.
4. Is the journal recap sufficient after a long absence, or does the game need a short "previously" cinematic?
5. Does the town's moral ambiguity read as nuance, or as evasion? Test with players who have no attachment to the genre.
